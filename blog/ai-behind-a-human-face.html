Don't Reply to a Human Face When an AI Is Behind the Conversation

The growing problem of AI impersonating human communication

November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Imagine receiving a message on LinkedIn from someone whose profile includes a real name, a professional photograph, a job title, and a credible employment history.

You respond because you believe you're talking to that person.

You spend time writing a thoughtful reply, asking questions, sharing opinions, or perhaps discussing a business opportunity.

But what if the person never actually wrote the message? What if an AI system generated it, read your response, and continued the conversation automatically?

You thought you were talking to a human. In reality, you were interacting with software.

And nobody told you.

A Real Profile Doesn't Guarantee a Real Conversation

Today, AI systems can generate personalized messages, respond to emails, manage LinkedIn conversations, and even maintain long-running discussions.

The concerning part isn't the technology itself.

It's the deception.

When someone uses their real identity and photograph but delegates their conversations entirely to AI without informing the other person, they create a false impression of personal interaction.

The recipient believes someone is listening, thinking, and responding.

But that might not be happening at all.

Your Time Deserves Respect

Communication requires time and attention.

When I respond to a message from a real person, I'm investing my time in what I believe is a human interaction.

If the other side is simply an automated system designed to keep me engaged, generate leads, or collect information, I deserve to know.

My time is human. My attention is human. I have the right to know whether the conversation is human too.

This becomes especially important in professional communication, where trust and authenticity matter.

AI Assistance Is Not the Same as AI Impersonation

There's an important distinction.

Using AI to improve grammar, translate a message, or help draft an email is perfectly reasonable. The person still decides what to communicate and takes responsibility for the message.

But allowing AI to independently initiate conversations, read replies, make decisions, and continue communicating under a human identity is different.

One is assistance.

The other can become impersonation when it is deliberately concealed.

Transparency Should Be the Minimum Standard

I'm not against AI communication. AI assistants can save time, improve productivity, and make communication more efficient.

But transparency should be non-negotiable.

If an AI is managing a conversation, the recipient should be informed.

A simple disclosure would be enough:

“This account uses an AI assistant to manage and respond to messages.”

Then the recipient can decide whether to continue.

That is a matter of informed choice and mutual respect.

My Personal Rule

I don't want to spend my time building what I believe is a human connection with a system pretending to be that person.

If someone wants to communicate with me through an AI assistant, that's their choice.

But I should have a choice too.

Don't hide automation behind a human face.

AI should help us communicate better, not make us question whether anyone is actually on the other side of the conversation.

The future of communication will certainly involve AI.

But the future of trust depends on knowing who — or what — we're talking to.
